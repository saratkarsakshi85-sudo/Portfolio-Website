Key Takeaways

  1. Outdated Patterns, Avoid
    Some traditional, busy patterns feel outdated for 2026. Focus on cleaner, organic and contemporary patterns, especially in 300×600 mm formats.
  2. Travertine, Take Forward
    A strong 2026 trend, particularly in large formats such as 1200×2400 mm. Travertine-look tiles bring a warm, natural beige feel with the practicality of tile for bathrooms and wet areas.
  3. Marble + 3D Ceramic, Take Forward
    White, beige, grey and Marfil marble remain commercially strong, along with fluted marble, especially in large formats (1200×2400 / 1200×1800 mm). 3D ceramic tiles, mainly 300×600 mm, add depth and work well as feature-wall accents.

What I would take from this for Material Depot

Bathroom

  • 300×600 mm: strongest format for decorative/3D ceramic wall tiles.
  • 600×1200 mm: good for contemporary tactile and marble surfaces.
  • 1200×2400 mm: premium travertine/marble large-format opportunity.
  • Finish: prioritise matt, stone, travertine and refined 3D textures over overly glossy surfaces.

Kitchen

  • 300×600 mm: ideal for backsplash and decorative wall applications.
  • 600×1200 mm: premium, cleaner-looking large-format option.
  • Finish: prioritise glossy/easy-clean surfaces, followed by matt, subway, terrazzo and subtle textures.
  • Design: keep patterns more controlled because the kitchen already has cabinets, countertops and appliances competing visually.

Research Conclusion

Discover
Across the store visits, some recurring patterns were seen in different stores. Subway, KitKat and tactile tiles were repeatedly available across stores, showing a strong current market presence. Panel tiles were also repeatedly seen, especially tropical, nature-inspired and decorative designs. Moroccan, Indian and traditional motifs appeared across multiple stores, showing that traditional patterns are still relevant. Earthy, muted and neutral colours were more recurring than bright colours. Marble and stone-look finishes, especially white, beige and bold-veined designs, were commonly available. Stores were often grouping similar tiles together to help customers understand how they can be used in a space.

Define
The research shows a clear mix of modern + traditional + natural design preferences. Users seem to prefer a simple base with one statement element, rather than using strong patterns everywhere. At the same time, AI is becoming part of the design process, allowing users to explore colours, tiles, materials and styles before making decisions. This creates an opportunity to make the tile-selection process more visual, simple and personalised for everyday users.

Overall Finding
The recurring trend is: simple base + texture/pattern + natural or earthy colour. This balance between minimal spaces and expressive feature tiles appears across both the physical market and the way users are exploring designs through AI.
