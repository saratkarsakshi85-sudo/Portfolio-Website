Nurtr, wrapped up

What this project is about

Nurtr is a parent–teacher communication platform that turns scattered updates into one shared picture of a student’s progress. Grades, attendance, messages, and schedules live in role-aware experiences for parents, teachers, and admins.

  • 3Primary roles
  • 2Platforms designed

Design principles

  • Role-first clarity

    Parent, teacher, and admin paths diverge early so each person sees only what they need.

  • Trust around minors’ data

    Consent, permissions, and sensitive actions stay explicit—never buried in footnotes.

  • Attention before archive

    Ungraded work, unread messages, and missing attendance surface before dense history.

  • Shared language

    Desktop and mobile keep the same structure so families and teachers stay aligned.

Design hierarchy

  1. 01Needs attention

    Urgent tasks and alerts lead every home screen.

  2. 02Quick access

    Gradebook, attendance, messages, and schedule sit as immediate destinations.

  3. 03Day structure

    Schedules and class context frame the work without burying it.

  4. 04Account & settings

    Profile and configuration stay present but secondary.

Nurtr’s hierarchy protects what matters most: timely action, transparent consent, and a single story of how a student is doing.

Thank you for reading

Nurtr began with a simple observation that parent–teacher communication in Indian schools lives scattered across WhatsApp threads and paper diaries. It ends here, as a role-symmetric, India-first product built to bring that conversation into one calm, structured home.

  • RGrounded in research

    Every screen traces back to secondary research on Indian board systems, WhatsApp-heavy habits, and NEP 2020 compliance needs.

  • SBuilt for two roles at once

    Parent and teacher experiences mirror each other in structure, so switching roles never means relearning the app.

  • CDesigned to be lived in

    A warm, paper-cut collage identity across desktop and mobile built to feel human in a category full of grey ERPs.
